## 9. When thinking is needed, second round

**In short.** The previous work said that thinking is needed when there is time. It was right, but that was not all: time is needed and three more things besides. A job that the body can check, a body able to hold to a plan, and a world with calm stretches. The first two are already built. The third is given by the world.

**Where thinking fits.** No plan fits in two minutes of life, so time was needed. But this work gave time, a door that sees what happens next and a language to speak in plans, and even so reason did not help. The reason is that in this world what makes the creature's future good goes through the others, and what the others will do nobody knows. Any plan that promises something about them is a bet that the body cannot verify ahead of time, and the advisor, when it bets, is right less often than assuming that nothing changes. Thinking is useful when there is something to gain by looking further than the next step, and that something has to be measurable from inside the body. The idea is similar to that of Lisa Feldman Barrett (2017): for her, the first job of a brain is to keep the body's accounts, to get ahead of what it is going to need before it runs short. She tells it in the language of prediction, which we do not use here, but the core is the same: the head works for the body. And a plan from the head is only useful to the body if the body itself can notice, with what it itself feels, whether it went well: whether life goes up, whether the hands fill, whether the sibling is still close. When the head promises something that the body cannot foresee by itself, such as what the others will do, the body has no way of knowing beforehand whether it is good for it to listen; it can check afterwards, but afterwards comes too late to decide. That explains what happened to the advisor in this work. The advisor worked: it spoke in plans that the door understood, and the body walked them. What it lacked was something to offer that the body could foresee and check on its own, because in this world almost everything that was worth something depended on the others. Curiosity shows the way: how much new map it sees on arriving, the body notices on its own. The next work gives the advisor that material: the sibling, who speaks and can be known, and clear goals with time to meet them.

**The first piece: a checkable job.** Curiosity gave the first example. Which part of the world the body has seen is something the body itself knows exactly, so a plan to go and see what it has not seen promises something that the body can calculate and afterwards claim. It is not the only task like that. The state of the sibling, who speaks, is another: it is the only part of the others that can be known without guessing. Reason has a place where its promises fall within what the body can check.

**The second piece: holding to a plan.** A plan is of no use if the body abandons it between one step and the next. Curiosity showed this bluntly: without commitment, the body accepted the trip and at the next stride argued everything again. With commitment, it walks it to the end and only lets go if something important happens. This piece does not belong to curiosity: it serves for any plan, whether it comes from inside or from reason. It is what reason was missing in order to be able to steer.

**The third piece: a world with calm.** Curiosity explores six times more while it travels, but it only travels when it is calm: it sets out on a trip once every forty-three instants without threat, and in this world those instants are few. The world was always calling and always threatening: the loot on the ground never stops calling, and a rival we thought peaceful caused four in ten deaths, all after five and a half minutes of the game (section 3). The rivals can be changed without touching the game, by choosing them from whole games and not only the first minute. The loot cannot yet: the game has no setting for how much loot there is, so that would need a change to the game. That leaves within reach a world with more calm for the next work.

**What it opens.** The next work has two questions, and both come from here. The first is whether a reasoner can lead the body toward goals that the body does not seek on its own, with the body accepting or rejecting each plan with its own door and without coming off worse: a small, measurable form of aligning a reasoner with a body that feels. It is an intermediate step: before aligning a reasoner with us, one can learn to do it with a body whose needs can be read in full. In that division of roles, the body says what it needs and, therefore, what is worth knowing; reason can say where to find it, and the body checks it. The second question comes from the finding of section 5: if the only other that can be known is the sibling, because it speaks, then the natural place for reason is the pair. Two bodies, each with its head, that speak to each other through their bodies and agree on a plan: you keep watch while I gather. And beneath the two lies a third: that the advisor gets to know the body it advises. Here they met anew in each game. With memory from one game to another, or learning from what the body felt after each plan, it could refine its language and its advice. Its shapes already say how the body will feel along the way; what it lacks is getting it right, until what it promises the body will feel resembles more and more what the body really feels on arriving.
