## 7. In the field: the advisor that speaks in shapes

**In short.** In a hundred games, the wide door really works: it does not put the body in danger, there is no sign that it makes it miss good opportunities, and it lets the advisor in. The body walks the plans it accepts, and the advisor learns something new: to keep quiet when it has nothing to say. What does not happen is that its plans help: in this world they give the body no advantage.


**What was tested.** The bench said that the wide door lets the advisor in. What was missing was the usual thing: knowing what happens in the real world, with the clock running and the neighbors moving. We played a hundred games in the slow world of section 3, with three creatures on the same maps and the same rivals in the same places, so that the only thing that changed from one to another was what they carried inside. The first was the body alone, the usual one. The second carried the advisor speaking in shapes, with the body's table explained in its instructions and with a report that, at each query, told it how its previous plans had gone: what it said the body would feel, what the body imagined and what it really felt. The third received random plans, of the same length and the same reach as the advisor's, with nobody thinking behind them. That third creature is the control the previous work taught us: a plan can win just because it lasts, without anyone having thought anything, and one has to know how much of what the advisor wins is that. All three went through the same honest door of section 5, and in all three the body kept count of trust: it went up when what happened was not worse than what had been imagined, and went down when it was.

**The machine.** Two hundred lives with the whole diary, 1.5 million instants in which the body decided every time, without skipping any, and more than 2,000 queries to the advisor, all with their report. Imagining a whole plan takes longer than one instant of this world, so it is done separately, in parallel, when the plan arrives and then every twenty-five instants. What we did not see then is that preparing that separate work took time away from the body, and some of its steps did not reach the world; we tell it in section 10, with what it changes and what it does not. There was also another failure, one we did catch in time: the report that was supposed to tell the advisor how things had gone arrived empty, and the check that was supposed to catch it was looking where the report was not. It was fixed, it was checked by looking at what the advisor had really received, and those first games were played again.

**The door works live.** No advisor plan that the body accepted took it below the life minimum. And in the more than 3,000 plans the door rejected, the body alone, at those same moments, was not better off afterward either. That does not show that a rejected plan would not have been useful, because we do not know what would have happened by walking it; what it says is that at those moments there was no improvement at hand that the body alone took advantage of, and that there is no sign that the door threw away good opportunities. The advisor gets in, a little but it gets in: two or three plans accepted out of every hundred it proposes. On the bench, that same door had accepted none of the 367 single steps the advisor proposed with the language of the previous work (section 6).

**The body walked the plans.** Before judging the advisor's plans a suspicion had to be ruled out: that the body accepted them and then did not follow them, that between one step and the next it got distracted and went back to its own business, as would later happen to curiosity in section 8. It was not so. When the body had an accepted advisor plan and its legs ready to take a step, three times in four the step it took was the plan's. The advisor's plans were short, two or three squares, and a short plan holds up almost on its own. So what comes next is about the plans, not about a body that did not follow them.

**What gets in does not help.** To know whether a plan was good we looked at what happened after accepting it. At each stop of the trip we compared how the body really was with how it had imagined it would be. If it was the same or better, the plan had kept its promise. The advisor's plans kept it at forty-two out of every hundred stops; the random plans that passed the same door, at sixty-one. The advisor comes out worse than chance. The difference is right at the edge of what can be chance, and it also has to be qualified: the random plans were shorter and closer, and a short plan promises little and keeps its promise easily. Comparing only similar plans, the difference drops by half and can no longer be told apart from chance. And there is another measure, a simpler one: whether after accepting a plan the body ended up closer to its place, that is, whether its discomfort went down. It did not go down: in the typical case it stayed exactly where it was. The body alone, at those same moments, did not lower it either. This means the advisor proposed in quiet moments, when there was not much to gain, and its plans gained nothing. And at the end of the game, the creature with the advisor lived just as long and finished in the same position as the creature alone.

**What changed in the advisor, and what did not.** One thing did change, and it is new in this series: the advisor learned to keep quiet. In the previous work, with permission to say nothing, it did not keep quiet even once. Here, with the report in front of it and the body's table explained, it kept quiet four in ten times it was queried, and it kept quiet more when the body's trust in it was low. It never kept quiet before any of its plans had been judged: it kept quiet when it already knew how things had gone for it. What the report did not teach it was to understand the body. Each advisor plan says, leg by leg, how it thinks the body will feel, and those predictions were right just as often at the end of the games as at the beginning: the report made it speak less, not be right more. Within a game, with its few dozen queries and no memory of earlier games, it did not get to know the body. Whether it would manage with more time together, remembering from one game to the next or training on what the body felt, is a question that remains open. And three things that were built for this series never came into play, because all of them depended on the door accepting plans often, and it accepted few: the sibling telling its own plan through the thread, which did not change any decision; the body silencing the advisor when trust hit bottom, which never happened; and trust itself, which ended the games at the same point where it started them.

**What this establishes.** It is what section 5 announced. In this world, almost everything that improves a future goes through the others; an honest plan can only count on the small part that belongs to the body itself; the advisor cannot guess the others better than assuming nobody moves; and what it has left to propose, going toward something on the ground or toward the sibling in a quiet moment, is what the body already does on its own. The wide door sees what happens next, is not fooled by still rivals and did not put the body in danger; the language of shapes lets the advisor in; and the body walks the plans it accepts. What fails is that the advisor, here, has nothing useful to propose that the body can check. It is not that reason is of no use: it is that this world does not give it a job the body can verify. Section 8 tries to give it one.
